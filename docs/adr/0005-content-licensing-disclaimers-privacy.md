# Content licensing, advice disclaimers, and privacy — staged alpha→public posture

Exposing AI-generated travel content and third-party images to users creates legal exposure we handle with a **staged posture**: defensible-for-private-alpha now, hardened before public launch.

**Image licensing (amends ADR 0002).** SearXNG returns scraped, hotlinked third-party images. "We don't store, only display" lowers risk (the US *Perfect 10* "server test" gives real cover for inline-linking) but does **not** zero it — display is its own right, the server test is rejected in some circuits (*Goldman v. Breitbart*) and weaker outside the US, and re-presenting a curated image inside a commercial product is not neutral search. For the **private allowlist alpha**: keep SearXNG but **hotlink-only (never store), show source attribution, and provide a takedown/contact path**. Before **public launch** (hard gate): flip to **Wikimedia-primary** (already wired, CC-licensed, real-place photos, good fit) with SearXNG as attributed fallback. Either path requires adding `license`/`author`/`attribution` fields to the image evidence record and rendering a credit line — so that work lands regardless.

**Advice disclaimers.** Model prompt-behavior ("mark estimates," "verify before booking") is probabilistic and not a legal shield, so protection is product-level, in three layers: **(1)** a blanket ToS/disclaimer at signup (AI-generated, may be inaccurate, not professional travel/legal/medical advice, no liability); **(2)** a persistent low-noise footer caveat; **(3)** inline point-of-use "AI estimate — confirm with official sources" markers scoped **only** to high-risk categories — **visa, health, safety/advisories, emergency info, and any monetary figure**. Scoping inline markers to those categories both targets the facts most likely to cause harm and avoids disclaimer fatigue that would make users ignore all of them.

**Privacy.** Templated policy is acceptable for alpha, but two parts are product-specific and hand-written: **(1)** trip data — including travel dates, budget, and group composition (kids' ages) — is sent to third parties (the user's LLM provider incl. free-tier Gemini which *may train on inputs*, search providers, Wikimedia); the policy names them and onboarding warns free-tier keys aren't private; **(2)** children's-data minimization — collect only rough ages the plan needs, not names/DOB. Lawyer review is gated before public launch (not required for the private alpha), consistent with the image-licensing gate.
